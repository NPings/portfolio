Generative & evaluative research · HP Inc. · 2021

HP Wolf Security

A diary study on an unreleased device protection build found that self-managed security asked too much of the average user — and reshaped the product around built-in IT support instead.

RoleLead researcher
MethodDiary study
DurationLongitudinal
CompanyHP Inc.

The build tested in this study predates HP Wolf Security's public release and was never publicly available. No screenshots exist for this case study at this time.

01

The question

HP was building a new device protection suite — antivirus and VPN functionality — before any of it was public. The open question: could an everyday user actually manage their own device security, or did the product need to assume less from them?

02

What I did

Ran a longitudinal diary study with participants using an unreleased build on provided laptops. Assigned structured tasks that exercised real product functionality over time, then held regular debriefs with each participant to surface friction as it happened — not just recall it after the fact.

03

What we learned

Self-management was too involved for the average user. The friction wasn't about interface polish — it was a mismatch between what the product asked of people and what people actually wanted to be responsible for.

04

What changed

The product was reshaped around built-in IT support rather than pure self-service — a structural shift, not a UI tweak, and one that traced directly back to this study.
